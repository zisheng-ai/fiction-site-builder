# Survival Games, Death Challenges, and Human Hunts

Use this reference for sealed estates, coerced elimination games, wealthy spectators, deadly challenges, or participants hunted for entertainment or leverage.

## Story Promise

The protagonist is told survival requires accepting the organizer's rules. The strongest arc moves from learning the game, to discovering what the game measures, to breaking the economic or social system that makes spectatorship possible.

## Conflict Engine

- Define entry coercion, arena boundaries, monitoring, resources, scoring, penalties, audience, and organizer objective.
- Every rule needs an enforcement mechanism and a loophole with cost.
- Give participants independent histories, skills, secrets, and exit preferences.
- Make alliances materially useful and morally unstable; betrayal must change the field.
- The organizer needs a goal beyond sadism: succession, blackmail, research, debt collection, political selection, or evidence destruction.

## Escalation Architecture

1. Open after entry, at the first rule demonstration or impossible choice.
2. The protagonist discovers a discrepancy between announced and actual rules.
3. A participant's independent plan causes an irreversible loss.
4. The group secures a false victory that expands surveillance or stakes.
5. Midpoint: the arena is not closed, the audience is not passive, or elimination serves a second operation.
6. Split alliances over escape versus exposing the organizers.
7. Transfer control of one feed, gate, resource, or betting mechanism.
8. Climax makes spectators, sponsors, or beneficiaries visible and legally vulnerable.

## Character System

Use the protagonist, credible rival, reluctant ally, rule expert, prior-cycle survivor, compromised staff member, organizer's heir or proxy, and vulnerable participant who still has agency. Do not create disposable bodies only for shock.

## Public Reversal

The protagonist should force the organizer to act outside their own rules on a witnessed channel. Survival and exposure must collide; neither should arrive free.

## Safety and Failure Modes

- Avoid graphic gore as the primary escalation tool.
- Do not provide replicable trap construction or evasion instructions.
- Do not rely on arbitrary rule changes; foreshadow system limits.
- Romance cannot erase coercion between captor and participant.

## Quality Gate

The story passes when rules stay internally consistent, participants alter one another's choices, reversals change the game model, and the finale attacks the audience and financing system as well as the arena.
