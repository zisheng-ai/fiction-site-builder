# Workplace Persecution and Industry Blacklisting

Use this reference for stories about credit theft, fabricated misconduct, abusive managers, malicious termination, professional retaliation, or coordinated blacklisting.

## Story Promise

The workplace controls the protagonist's income and professional identity, then turns process into punishment. Satisfaction comes from making the institution apply its own standards to the people who manipulated them. A new job offer is not a climax; the record must be corrected and the scheme exposed.

## Conflict Engine

- Define the valuable work being stolen: patent, campaign, codebase, client strategy, research, design, or revenue account.
- Make retaliation procedural: access revocation, performance plans, compliance reports, references, licensing complaints, or vendor pressure.
- Give every document a creator, timestamp, custodian, and vulnerability. Screenshots alone should not carry the case.
- Split opposition across a manager, peer beneficiary, HR/legal gatekeeper, and executive sponsor.
- Give the protagonist an ethical constraint: exposing the scheme may harm clients, users, patients, or junior staff.

## Escalation Architecture

1. Open during a public credit theft, disciplinary meeting, or termination.
2. The protagonist preserves one proof object before access disappears.
3. An internal appeal becomes a trap that expands the allegations.
4. A coworker helps, then withdraws when their own livelihood is threatened.
5. Midpoint: the protagonist proves the work was hers but learns the company has coordinated external blacklisting.
6. She builds an outside coalition: regulator, client, union, journalist, auditor, or competitor with mixed motives.
7. The antagonist offers reinstatement in exchange for silence.
8. The climax combines a public product event, board inquiry, arbitration, or licensing hearing with authenticated evidence.

## Character System

Use the targeted professional, a compromised manager, a peer who benefited, a junior employee at risk, an institutional gatekeeper, an external investigator, and an ally whose career conflicts with loyalty. At least two supporting characters must act without either lead present.

## Public Reversal

The best payoff replays the original humiliation under reversed evidence conditions. The protagonist should name authorship, sequence, and consequence precisely. Avoid a vague viral speech; show who loses authority, access, title, license, or control.

## Safety and Failure Modes

- HR is neither magical protection nor a single villain; write incentives and jurisdiction accurately.
- Do not resolve systemic retaliation through a CEO's romantic favor.
- Avoid implausible instant lawsuits. Use preservation, discovery, regulators, arbitration, or negotiated leverage with time costs.
- Do not make every colleague cruel. Fear, self-interest, and partial courage create stronger conflict.

## Quality Gate

The story passes when the stolen work is understandable, retaliation escalates across at least three mechanisms, evidence survives scrutiny, and the final victory restores more than employment.
