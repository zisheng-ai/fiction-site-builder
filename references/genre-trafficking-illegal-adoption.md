# Trafficking, Illegal Adoption, and Stolen Identity Networks

Use this reference for infant sale, fraudulent orphanages, coercive adoption, cross-border identity trade, missing-person registries, or adults discovering that their legal family history was manufactured.

## Story Promise

The protagonist follows one stolen identity into a market protected by respectable institutions. The story must center victims and survivors as people with competing needs, not proof objects. Resolution requires both personal recognition and disruption of the network's repeatable mechanism.

## Conflict Engine

- Build the network through recruitment, document creation, payment, placement, oversight failure, and reputation laundering.
- Distinguish birth relatives, adoptive relatives, brokers, institutions, and adult survivors; do not flatten them into two moral camps.
- Give identity evidence limits across jurisdictions, languages, sealed records, and time.
- Make disclosure ethically costly: a truth may free one person while destabilizing another's chosen family or safety.
- Use investigators, lawyers, journalists, registrars, or survivor groups with bounded authority.

## Escalation Architecture

1. Open with a document, match, or reunion that contains an impossible fact.
2. A narrow search reveals a repeated identifier across unrelated cases.
3. The protagonist contacts someone who refuses the truth for a credible reason.
4. A respectable institution destroys or seals access.
5. Midpoint: the apparent individual crime becomes a still-active cross-border system.
6. Survivors disagree over exposure, prosecution, privacy, and family contact.
7. An insider offers records in exchange for immunity or protection.
8. Climax through coordinated preservation, public accountability, and safe extraction—not vigilante spectacle.

## Character System

Use the searching protagonist, another survivor with different goals, a birth or adoptive relative, broker or fixer, institutional gatekeeper, registry or lab specialist, legal advocate, and compromised insider. Give survivors control over naming and contact.

## Evidence and Public Payoff

Combine civil records, financial transfers, metadata, travel or custody documents, DNA where consensual, and testimony. One match cannot establish the whole network. The public payoff should preserve privacy while proving a repeatable scheme.

## Safety and Failure Modes

- Never depict trafficked people as commodities in narrative voice; only perpetrators use dehumanizing language.
- Do not imply all international or private adoption is trafficking.
- Avoid graphic exploitation and operational instructions.
- Do not force reunification as the only emotionally valid ending.

## Quality Gate

The story passes when the network has credible logistics, survivors disagree with agency, proof crosses more than one source, and the ending offers multiple forms of family and remedy.
