# Digital Blackmail, Deepfakes, and Networked Humiliation

Use this reference for non-consensual recordings, deepfakes, livestream humiliation, doxxing, anonymous extortion, coordinated harassment, or synthetic evidence used to destroy a person's identity.

## Story Promise

The protagonist faces content that spreads faster than truth and an audience trained to treat visibility as guilt. The story succeeds when she stops chasing every copy, identifies the system coordinating amplification, and creates a public verification event the antagonist cannot edit after the fact.

## Conflict Engine

- Define the asset under attack: job, custody, election, license, contract, relationship, or physical safety.
- Separate content creation, distribution, monetization, platform enforcement, and offline coercion.
- Make attribution difficult but not magical. Metadata, payment trails, access logs, source media, witnesses, and behavioral timing must combine.
- Give the protagonist a reason not to release all evidence immediately: privacy, another victim, an active investigation, or proof integrity.
- Let public allies have incentives and failure modes; virality is not justice.

## Escalation Architecture

1. Open during a livestream, public screening, or message demanding action before release.
2. The protagonist proves one artifact is false, but correction amplifies it.
3. A second leak uses real material, making blanket denial impossible.
4. Platform or employer process is weaponized against her.
5. Midpoint: the campaign is a paid network serving a larger takeover, cover-up, or identity theft.
6. An ally's account or device becomes the apparent source.
7. Build a synchronized proof chain across source media, money, distribution, and live witness.
8. Climax during a broadcast, hearing, launch, or vote where the antagonist initiates the final release and authenticates their own control.

## Character System

Use the targeted protagonist, campaign architect, technical operator, public beneficiary, journalist or creator with mixed incentives, platform or legal gatekeeper, prior target, and ally whose compromised access creates relational cost.

## Evidence and Public Payoff

Do not claim pixels alone prove authorship. Establish what is manipulated, who had source access, how distribution was purchased, and who benefits. The public reversal must be short, demonstrable, and followed by enforceable consequences.

## Safety and Failure Modes

- Do not reproduce sexualized abusive media in detail or use it as cover titillation.
- Avoid step-by-step deepfake, account compromise, or harassment instructions.
- Do not imply a victim owes the public intimate proof of authenticity.
- A counter-harassment mob is not a clean victory.

## Quality Gate

The story passes when online and offline stakes interact, real and synthetic material complicate denial, attribution uses multiple sources, and the protagonist regains authorship without surrendering privacy.
