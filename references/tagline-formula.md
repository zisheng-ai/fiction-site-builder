# Tagline Formula — Fiction Facebook Ad Copy

Load this reference whenever writing or optimizing book taglines for Facebook ad copy, site book cards, or any paid-traffic context. This is the primary tagline authority; `facebook-ads.md` defers to this file for all copy rules.

---

## Why taglines are mission-critical

The tagline is the single highest-leverage variable in fiction ad performance. It determines whether someone stops scrolling, reads chapter 1, and becomes an ad-monetized reader. The cover stops the scroll; the tagline closes the click. Neither alone is sufficient.

**The economics:** CTR improvement from 1% → 2% halves your CPC. On a $30/day campaign that matters more than any audience optimization. Tagline quality directly gates profitability.

---

## Meta compliance — the hard limits

Dark romance and paranormal fiction sit in a policy gray zone. The rule is: **text can go further than images**. Facebook's automated review prioritizes visuals. Explicit copy is rarely flagged unless paired with an explicit image.

### Allowed
- Emotional intensity: *obsession, possession, consume, devour, claim, ruin, destroy, dark, dangerous, forbidden*
- Power dynamics: *billionaire, warlord, enemy, captor, professor, demon king, fae lord*
- Implied intimacy: *desire, hunger, craving, what he does to her, what she does to him*
- Forbidden framing: *what they shouldn't want, what the contract didn't cover, what neither of them admits*
- Supernatural threat: *marked, bound, tethered, claimed, hunted*

### Banned (triggers review or rejection)
- Explicit sexual acts described directly
- Exposed genitalia or sex acts in ad image
- "18+" labels — counterproductive, signals adult content to reviewers
- Non-consensual scenarios framed as desirable (different from dark romance tension)
- Misleading claims about the story that cannot be delivered

### The line in practice
*"Dangerous. Possessive. Completely devoted to her."* — passes.
*"He'll ruin her in every way."* — passes (tension, not act).
Explicit description of what happens behind closed doors — fails.

**The safe combination:** intensity words in copy + alluring-but-clothed cover image. Neither element triggers review alone; together they outperform explicit material because Facebook delivers them to broader audiences at lower CPM.

## The 5 psychological trigger formulas

These are the highest-CTR patterns confirmed across romance fiction Facebook advertising. Apply by genre match — not all formulas work equally for every sub-genre.

---

### Formula A — Forbidden Identity Combo

**When to use:** Dark romance, mafia, billionaire, paranormal. Any story where the hero's role makes the relationship dangerous.

**Structure:**
```
He's my [forbidden role]. He's also [complicating truth].
```

**Mechanism:** Reader instantly maps the power gap and the risk. "Forbidden role" creates tension; "complicating truth" adds a layer that makes it feel like a real story, not a trope.

**Examples:**
- "He's my captor. He's also the only one keeping me alive."
- "He's my enemy's brother. My handler. My obsession."
- "My warden. The only man who knows what the contract actually says."

**Dark romance variant (A+D hybrid — highest performer for velvet-throne):**
```
[Dark trait]. [Shocking contradiction]. [Completely devoted].
```
- "Ruthless. Possessive. Completely devoted to her."
- "Dangerous. Obsessive. And absolutely certain she belongs to him."

---

### Formula B — Curiosity Gap

**When to use:** All genres. The default formula when the plot has one devastating reversal.

**Structure:**
```
[Normal-seeming premise]. [One fact that changes everything — cut before resolution].
```

**Mechanism:** The reader's brain cannot tolerate an open information loop. The cut at the highest-tension moment is the click.

**Examples:**
- "She applied for the job. He already had her file — annotated two years before the posting went live."
- "Six weeks venting to a stranger about her nightmare boss. Then she found his business card. The stranger was her boss — and he'd known for two weeks."
- "The contract ends in two weeks. He knows. She knows. Neither of them has brought it up."
- "She signed the lease on Tuesday. By Wednesday there was a demon in her apartment and a very specific feeling he'd been waiting for her."

**Opening 8-word starters that fire curiosity gap:**
- "He'd been watching her for..."
- "She signed. She didn't read clause..."
- "The channel had been dead for..."
- "Every [woman/Scholar/diplomat] who came before..."
- "She thought the [scholarship/job/contract] meant..."

---

### Formula C — Reader Manifesto ("For girls who...")

**When to use:** Cold traffic targeting BookTok/romance reader communities. Works as the secondary ad copy after a cover image — not as the cover's caption.

**Structure:**
```
For [reader identity]. This one's for you.
```

**Mechanism:** Identity mirror. The reader recognizes herself in the description and feels the book was made for her specifically.

**Examples:**
- "For the girls who want the villain to win."
- "For girls who root for the obsessive hero, not the safe one."
- "If you skip to the spicy chapters, this one's for you."
- "Dark. Morally grey. Absolutely unputdownable."

**Note:** Formula C is a targeting signal as much as a tagline. Use it to reach niche audiences who already self-identify as dark romance readers. Less effective for broad audiences who don't know the genre.

---

### Formula D — Power Reversal

**When to use:** Revenge stories, enemies to lovers, any book where a supposedly weaker character has hidden leverage.

**Structure:**
```
[Dominant character's apparent advantage]. Until [unexpected reversal].
```
or
```
[Dark/threatening trait]. [Tender or unexpected contradiction].
```

**Mechanism:** The tension between power and vulnerability is the core engine of dark romance. Showing both in the same tagline makes the hero feel real, not just threatening.

**Examples:**
- "He controlled everything. Until her."
- "The demon king kneeled. For her."
- "He bought her father's company to bring her to her knees. She walked into his boardroom with a counteroffer — and his own files told him to be afraid of her."
- "Five hundred rituals. Not one woman survived. She did — and his power answered her instead of him."

---

### Formula E — Cliffhanger Micro-Copy

**When to use:** Any book with a strong opening scene or a devastating first reveal. Works best when the story's hook is a specific moment, not a setup.

**Structure:**
```
[High-stakes scene in present tense, cut at maximum tension —]
```
*Use an em dash or period at the cut. Never resolve.*

**Mechanism:** Reads like the first sentence of a novel. The reader is already inside the story before they realize they haven't clicked yet.

**Examples:**
- "The fresco had been sealed for 389 years. On day four, the figure inside stepped out — and said her full name."
- "She spent twenty years defending an empire built on a genocide she didn't know existed. Now she has to choose: save billions — or tell the truth."
- "The channel had been dead for thirty years. Then it transmitted a phrase he was never cleared to know — in his dead partner's voice."
- "Her mission was to kill him. He pulled her out of the wreckage instead — and never once asked why she was there."

## Formula selection by genre

| Genre | Best formula | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Dark romance (billionaire, mafia) | A+D hybrid | Power gap is the premise; contradiction is the pull |
| Paranormal / shifter | B or E | The "impossible" fact is the hook |
| Fae / dark fantasy | E then A | Scene-opener creates world; forbidden identity adds stakes |
| Demon / monster romance | B | Domestic normality broken by supernatural claim is the click |
| Fake dating / contract marriage | B | Expiration-date tension is formula B's native territory |
| Second chance romance | E | The unavoidable encounter is a single high-tension moment |
| Enemies to lovers | D | Power reversal is the sub-genre's core promise |
| Revenge romance | A | The disguise or false role is formula A's forbidden-role slot |
| Dark academia / secret society | B | Document or evidence is the proof object |
| Space opera / military sci-fi | E | High-stakes decision at the cut is the click |
| Sports romance / contemporary | B | The "he knew" reveal is the curiosity gap |

## Writing rules (universal, all formulas)

### Length
25–40 words. Mobile feeds show ~3 lines before "See more." The hook must land before that truncation.

### First 8 words
Must contain either a curiosity gap or an emotional punch. Test: cover everything after word 8 — does the reader want to keep going?

| Weak opening | Strong opening |
|---|---|
| "Elena Vargas has been running for months..." | "She's been running for eighteen months — and the man protecting her was there that night." |
| "When Nora Blake's sister disappeared..." | "Her sister vanished. The only lead: a sealed vault with a man who hadn't aged in six centuries." |
| "In a world where your score..." | "Her city score hit zero overnight. No identity, no housing, no appeal." |

### Final line
Always unresolved. Never conclude. The reader must feel *"and then what?"* as a physical pull.

| Weak final line | Strong final line |
|---|---|
| "...and they both fell in love." | "...and he hasn't brought it up." |
| "...but she couldn't resist him." | "...and never once asked why she was there." |
| "...everything changed between them." | "...and his own files told him to be afraid of her." |

### Sentence rhythm
Short sentences hit harder. Vary length: one longer setup, one shorter reversal, one short unresolved ending.

### Power words
These words earn their place because they imply withheld information:
*already knew / hasn't mentioned it / said nothing / recognized it / never brought it up / expecting her to / chose her deliberately / never asked why*

### What to avoid
- Generic adjectives alone: "brooding", "mysterious", "irresistible", "smoldering" — invisible to scrollers
- Telling the emotion: "their forbidden love burned bright" — say the situation, not the feeling
- More than one question mark — one rhetorical question hooks; two is a quiz
- Resolved endings — never explain what happens, only what is at stake

## Click-driven rewrite method (bulk optimization)

Use this 5-step pass when rewriting or auditing a site's full book list.

1. **Start with the unsafe fact, not the character.** First clause: danger, manipulation, disappearance, debt, betrayal, document, bloodline, impossible evidence. Never open with a name.

2. **Make first 8 words carry the click.** Good openers: *"Every woman who..."*, *"The contract already..."*, *"Her mother vanished..."*, *"The evidence named..."*, *"He bought..."*, *"The ritual required..."*, *"She signed..."*

3. **Turn the middle on a concrete proof object.** One physical thing: ledger, contract, recording, locked room, body, score, clause, blood sample, message, signature. This makes it feel like a story scene, not abstract copy.

4. **Reverse control.** The protagonist thought they were applying / investigating / negotiating / escaping / collecting / shutting something down. The second beat reveals they were selected / trapped / watched / claimed / framed / expected.

5. **End with withheld information.** One active secret, unresolved: who knew, why she was chosen, what he has not said, what the record hides, what happens when she refuses.

**Deslop mini-pass after writing:**
- Remove generic adjectives ("dark", "mysterious", "forbidden" as standalone, not as part of a specific claim)
- Cut passive voice
- Cut throat-clearing ("In a world where...", "Set against the backdrop of...")
- Cut endings that explain the emotion instead of leaving an unanswered threat

---

## Consistency rules across a book list

- No two books on the same site should share the same opener pattern (avoid "She thought..." × 5)
- Each tagline should imply a different proof object (not all contracts, not all sealed rooms)
- Check every tagline against the book description — the ad must not promise a scene the story cannot deliver
- Carousel and book-grid taglines should feel distinct enough that scrolling through them produces variety, not a template

---

## Quick checklist before publishing

- [ ] First 8 words: curiosity gap OR emotional punch
- [ ] 25–40 words total
- [ ] One clear reversal of power or expectation
- [ ] Ends unresolved — no conclusion, no emotional wrap-up
- [ ] No generic adjectives standing alone
- [ ] One concrete proof object in the middle
- [ ] Image and tagline imply the same scene without repeating each other
- [ ] No two adjacent books start with the same opener word
- [ ] Tagline matches what the story actually delivers
