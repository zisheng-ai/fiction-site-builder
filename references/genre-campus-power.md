# Campus Power, Elite Bullying, and Academic Retaliation

Use this reference for adult university or professional-school stories involving scholarship targeting, secret societies, faculty coercion, fabricated misconduct, recommendation blackmail, or institutional class power.

## Story Promise

The campus presents prestige as merit while privately deciding who may belong. The protagonist wins by exposing the machinery that converts donor preference and social cruelty into official records. Keep all central characters clearly adult.

## Conflict Engine

- Choose the controlled resources: scholarship, housing, lab access, internships, visa sponsorship, clinical placement, publication, or disciplinary status.
- Make bullying repeatable through a selection ritual, reporting pipeline, or patronage network.
- Separate social aggressors from administrators who make harm durable.
- Give the protagonist a skill relevant to the fight: law, metadata, journalism, organizing, science, accounting, or archival research.
- Protect other victims' agency; their files are not props for the protagonist's revenge.

## Escalation Architecture

1. Open with a public ritual designed to classify or humiliate the protagonist.
2. A complaint or appeal creates a more damaging official record.
3. The protagonist recognizes a pattern across prior targets.
4. An insider grants access but is implicated in building the system.
5. Midpoint: the archive is real, but publishing it would expose survivors.
6. Build consent-based testimony while the institution accelerates expulsion or blacklisting.
7. Force a donor, dean, society leader, and compromised ally into conflicting public statements.
8. Resolve through governance, accreditation, litigation, or collective action, not popularity alone.

## Character System

Use the targeted adult student, society heir, prior target, ambitious peer, faculty gatekeeper, donor representative, student journalist or advocate, and one ally with a competing need. At least four must materially change the plot twice.

## Public Reversal

The strongest scene is a hearing or ceremony where the institution expects confession. The protagonist controls the order of proof, preserves survivor consent, and makes the system's defenders authenticate their own pattern.

## Safety and Failure Modes

- Do not sexualize minors or blur ages; default to university, graduate, or professional-school adults.
- Avoid a single leaked video solving a distributed institution.
- Do not redeem an insider merely for handing over a key. Require testimony and material loss.
- Do not treat scholarships or working-class background as shameful.

## Quality Gate

The story passes when power operates through credible academic systems, victims retain control of their histories, and the public payoff changes policy, status, or governance.
