# Taiwan Serial Fiction: Oppression, Transaction, and Reversal

Use this reference for Taiwan-facing `zh-TW` serialized fiction that opens with a woman under family, marital, economic, institutional, or identity pressure and pays off through evidence, agency, and public reversal. This is a regional pacing and packaging guide, not a romance taxonomy. ## Primary Taxonomy

| Story engine | Primary genre | Relationship line | Required pressure guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engagement-night frame by a stepsister or relative | Domestic thriller / family abuse | Optional; the stranger may become ally, witness, or later partner | `genre-family-abuse.md` |

Do not label all of these `Romance`, `言情`, or `愛情小說`. Use the external conflict that controls the climax and victory condition. Load `genre-romance.md` only when the relationship itself is the primary conflict and resolution engine.

## Story Promise

The protagonist begins as someone a family or institution believes it can spend: a scapegoat, substitute, debt payment, traded bride, discarded caregiver, secret mother, or publicly rewritten heir. The serial promise is that she discovers what the transaction actually controls, secures independent leverage, exposes the people who priced her, and chooses any relationship only after she can safely refuse it.

## Taiwan-Facing Language

- Write natural Traditional Chinese throughout. Use `豪門`, `契約婚姻`, `替身`, `追妻`, `換親`, `身世`, `舊案`, `翻案`, `逆境翻身`, and `家族交易` only when they describe the actual book.
- Avoid reader-facing Mainland platform labels such as `霸总文`, `爽文`, `女频`, `网文`, or `年代文`.
- Keep names, institutions, money, law, medicine, and household details consistent with the setting. A Taiwan-set modern story uses Taiwan vocabulary and procedure; a fictional historical or period setting must not borrow modern Taiwan law accidentally.
- Favor short titles with a concrete pressure signal (`契`, `債`, `夜`, `婚`, `替`, `歸`, `換`, `囚`, `證`) rather than automatically adding a love signal.

## Conflict Engine

Define five elements before outlining:

1. **Who prices her:** stepfamily, foster family, spouse's family, employer, village elder, creditor, sect, or institution.
2. **What they receive:** debt relief, marriage alliance, shares, custody, housing, medical access, reputation, land, supplies, or erased liability.
3. **What proves it:** transfer agreement, hotel access record, remittance ledger, marriage deed, guardianship form, cassette, photograph provenance, medical file, or notarized will.
4. **Her independent need:** a dependent's safety, her name restored, ownership returned, a death investigated, professional independence, or community survival.
5. **Where denial becomes costly:** engagement banquet, wedding, family council, shareholder meeting, custody hearing, labor inquiry, trial, sect assembly, or public memorial.

Any ally who benefits from the transaction must surrender that benefit before trust or intimacy becomes credible. No single powerful character may be the protagonist's only exit route.

## 18–24 Chapter Escalation

1. Open inside the transaction: hotel door, wedding replacement, custody demand, debt signature, public abandonment, or identity denial.
2. Give the protagonist a tactical choice in Chapter 1; suffering without decision produces passivity, not suspense.
3. Reveal by Chapter 3 that the visible bargain covers a second asset, identity manipulation, or old crime.
4. Every 2–3 chapters, change a practical fact: access, money, housing, custody, evidence, witness loyalty, or public status.
5. At the first-quarter turn, offer one narrow exit. She returns only if a larger chosen goal requires it.
6. At midpoint, authenticate one proof object and make a supposed ally pay a cost.
7. After midpoint, let the opposition weaponize public morality: call her ungrateful, impure, greedy, disloyal, unstable, or dangerous.
8. Require structural change from an implicated ally: release a contract, return shares, testify, surrender custody leverage, or expose their own family.
9. Stage the climax where the original transaction or false identity was performed or celebrated.
10. End with enforceable consequences. A relationship may continue under fresh terms, but it is not the default prize.

## Chapter Rhythm

- **Opening 150–250 words:** an accusation, demand, document, door, or public act already in motion.
- **Middle:** one interpersonal pressure beat plus one material clue or competence beat. Never spend a full chapter only discussing feelings.
- **Turn:** reframe who knew, who paid, what the document controls, or which identity is false.
- **Hook-out:** cut on a name, seal, recording, key, test result, witness arrival, public refusal, or imminent action.

Alternate humiliation and competence. After every loss of status, show the protagonist observing, recording, bargaining, working, protecting someone, preserving evidence, or changing an alliance.

## General Cover Direction

- Use a vertical live-action short-drama event, not a posed couple.
- Show 3–4 readable adults, one active accusation or coercion gesture, and one proof object when the story has reached the evidence phase.
- For hotel-door stories, keep the protagonist alert and active. Never render intoxication or inability to consent as alluring.
- Keep faces clear in mid-key lighting. Use deep amber, burgundy, slate, emerald, or storm blue; avoid white haze, luxury-ad calm, and anonymous shadows swallowing the protagonist.

## Safety and Credibility

- Never depict drugging, assault, trafficking, document seizure, or inability to consent as a gift or proof of love.
- Do not repeat abuse only to make an ally look protective. Every abuse beat must change evidence, options, alliances, or public risk.
- A secret child is a person, not a paternity prop. Preserve the caregiver's agency and the child's safety.
- Do not forgive relatives because they cry at the climax. Tie consequences to law, property, employment, custody, community standing, or public record.
- Do not let a surprise heiress or awakened-power reveal erase earned competence.

## Quality Gate

The draft passes when the primary genre is named without defaulting to romance, the initial transaction can be stated in one sentence, the protagonist makes a consequential decision in Chapter 1, at least four active supporting characters hold independent leverage, the evidence chain has provenance and corroboration, abuse does not repeat without changing the board, and the ending leaves the protagonist able to say no without losing safety or livelihood.
