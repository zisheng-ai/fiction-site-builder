# Chinese Fiction Writing Profile

Use this file for Chinese-language chapters, Chinese titles, Chinese book descriptions, and Chinese prose review. It applies to both Simplified and Traditional Chinese at the writing-method level; `zh-TW` wording, typography, and regional reader-facing vocabulary remain governed by `internationalization.md`. It is not a translation target for English prose.

## Reader Contract

- Build immediate emotional legibility, relationship pressure, and consequence visibility without reducing every scene to a slogan.
- A chapter may move more quickly through beats than English prose, but each beat still needs a character choice, a concrete cost, and a changed power relation.
- Use close, active interiority. Let the viewpoint character register status, obligation, humiliation, desire, and danger through specific social details rather than imported English understatement.
- Dialogue may be more direct when the genre earns it, but no character should exist only to announce plot information or validate the protagonist.

## Scene and Chapter Shape

- Open on an immediate breach, demand, exposure, or unequal encounter. Establish who wants what and who can block it within the opening screen.
- Favor compact paragraphs and clean visual progression, but do not turn every line into a standalone cliffhanger. Paragraph breaks must track a new action, perception, speaker, or emotional turn.
- Use chapter titles that communicate the promise of the next conflict in natural Chinese. Avoid machine-translated English noun stacks and vague four-character solemnity with no plot signal.
- Escalate through visible social stakes: family standing, workplace authority, money, care duties, legal identity, public reputation, or bodily safety. The protagonist’s counteraction must be plausible for her resources.
- End on a concrete new fact, demand, deadline, reversal, or decision threshold. Do not rely on `她笑了`, `他后悔了`, or an empty declaration that the other side will pay.

## Emotional and Cultural Precision

- Do not import English romance beats as universal defaults. Chinese relationship tension may hinge on face, obligation, caregiving, class, kinship, public/private boundaries, or unequal access; make the relevant pressure explicit in the story world.
- Do not default every Chinese woman-led story to billionaire romance or `爽文`. Identify the external victory condition and preserve the protagonist’s independent competence.
- Match the regional brief. For `zh-TW`, use Traditional Chinese consistently and avoid Mainland-only reader-facing platform slang listed in `internationalization.md`. For `zh-CN`, use the requested register and never mix scripts casually.
- Emotional intensity must come from earned reversals and choices, not repeated exclamation marks, stacked reaction fragments, or characters declaring the moral of a scene.

## Chinese QA

Before A3 passes, verify:

1. The prose was composed for Chinese reading flow, not translated from English sentence order, idioms, or dialogue cadence.
2. The chapter has a readable progression of pressure → choice → consequence → hook-out, with no empty reaction-line padding.
3. Kinship terms, social address, money units, legal/workplace vocabulary, and regional wording are internally consistent.
4. Each high-emotion scene contains an observable action or changed relationship, not only inner judgment.
5. Titles, tagline, and synopsis use the target script and native reader language; `zh-TW` copy passes the Taiwan vocabulary checks in `internationalization.md`.
