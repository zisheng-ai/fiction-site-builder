# Entertainment Industry Coercion and Contract Blacklisting

Use this reference for exploitative talent contracts, role theft, image control, coerced access, replacement identities, manufactured scandal, or industry-wide blacklisting.

## Story Promise

The protagonist's public image is valuable precisely because other people own the machinery that creates it. She must separate audience attention from contractual control and expose how private coercion becomes respectable entertainment business.

## Conflict Engine

- Define who controls name, likeness, masters, credits, schedules, sponsorships, distribution, and crisis communications.
- Split the antagonist system among manager, producer, agency, publicist, platform, sponsor, and favored replacement.
- Give the protagonist work worth protecting; fame alone is a weak goal.
- Make every public statement create contractual or evidentiary consequences.
- Romance may complicate access and reputation but cannot become a more benevolent ownership deal.

## Escalation Architecture

1. Open at a premiere, audition, award, live show, or termination where credit is publicly reassigned.
2. The protagonist preserves original work or a contract discrepancy.
3. A denial campaign reframes her as unstable, difficult, or dangerous.
4. An ally benefits from replacing her and must choose whether to speak.
5. Midpoint: the scandal conceals systematic coercion involving multiple artists or projects.
6. Sponsors and distributors become leverage rather than neutral background.
7. The protagonist coordinates testimony while finishing or reclaiming the work.
8. Climax during a live or immutable public event where the system expects her compliance.

## Character System

Use the targeted artist, controlling representative, favored replacement, producer or executive sponsor, crew witness, journalist or critic, legal or union gatekeeper, and another artist with a conflicting survival strategy.

## Public Reversal

Mirror the original spectacle. The protagonist should control timing, authorship evidence, and the work itself; public applause matters less than rights, credits, contracts, and protected testimony changing hands.

## Safety and Failure Modes

- Do not make sexual coercion titillating or necessary for dramatic intensity.
- Avoid simplistic “cancel culture” framing when the plot is coordinated abuse or fraud.
- Do not resolve blacklisting through a billionaire partner buying the studio.
- Keep real celebrities and allegations out of fictionalized plots unless the user explicitly requests sourced nonfiction.

## Quality Gate

The story passes when the business mechanics are clear, the protagonist has a creative identity beyond victimhood, public narrative and legal ownership interact, and the ending returns control of work and name.
