# English Fiction Writing Profile

Use this file for English-language chapters, English titles, English book descriptions, and English prose review. It is not a translation target for Chinese serial prose.

## Reader Contract

- Prioritize causal clarity, scene immersion, and subtext over slogan-like emotional labeling.
- Let a paragraph earn one movement: perception, choice, consequence, or reversal. Do not force every paragraph into a punchline.
- Dialogue should carry conflict through what is avoided, revised, or deliberately misheard. Explain the hidden meaning only when the POV character needs it to make a decision.
- Use concrete agency: a person does something, another person resists, and the situation changes. Avoid abstract declarations of growth or power.

## Scene and Chapter Shape

- Open as near as possible to the destabilizing action. Orient readers through a specific physical detail, goal, or contested object rather than a setting inventory.
- Keep names and institutional nouns within the Chapter 1 cognitive-load limits in `story-long-write.md`.
- Vary sentence length naturally. Use short sentences for interruption, decision, or physical danger; do not stack fragments to manufacture intensity.
- English chapter titles should name a concrete pressure point, object, action, or irreversible choice. Avoid generic labels such as `Truth`, `Betrayal`, or `The Secret` unless the specificity is carried by the full title.
- End every chapter with an unanswered material consequence. Do not end on a translated web-serial catchphrase, a rhetorical question with an obvious answer, or a generic promise of revenge.

## Emotion and Relationship

- Show emotion through perception, behavior, withheld speech, and changed choices. A named emotion may be useful, but it cannot replace dramatization.
- Preserve ambiguity where the relationship needs it. English readers should be able to infer tension from competing objectives rather than being told every emotional conclusion.
- Romance is a subplot unless it is the primary conflict engine. Attraction cannot erase a character's independent goal, social obligation, or moral cost.

## English QA

Before A3 passes, verify:

1. The prose is idiomatic English rather than literal Chinese syntax, proverb cadence, or translated platform slang.
2. Paragraphing is scene-led, not line-break-led; isolated one-line emphasis is rare and earned.
3. Interior commentary does not restate what dialogue or action already proves.
4. The synopsis reads as a self-contained English premise, not a beat-by-beat serial blurb.
5. The chapter’s emotional and external turns remain understandable if all emphatic typography is removed.
