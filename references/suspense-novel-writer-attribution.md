## Original rules (key sections, translated)

### 10 Suspense Techniques (original taxonomy)

1. Information gap (Reader > Character; Character > Reader; parallel narration)
2. Time limits (countdown, survival pressure, truth-before-dawn)
3. Unknown threat (unseen enemy; unknowable danger)
4. Unreliable narration (purposeful omission; subjective bias; memory error; multiple accounts)
5. Foreshadowing (visible; invisible; double-meaning; mirror/contrast)
6. Misdirection (red herring; obvious suspect; apparent motive; coincidental timing)
7. Twist design (reasonable surprise; emotional impact; plot consequence; setup density)
8. Rhythm control (fast open; tension/release development; dense climax; emotional close)
9. Character craft (protagonist competence; motivation-driven behavior; antagonist intelligence)
10. Clue design (distributed; naturally embedded; multi-layer; logical chain)

### 8 Common Pitfalls (original list)

1. Logic holes — deduction not supported by planted evidence
2. Mid-section drag — no net progress across consecutive chapters
3. Character-as-tool — supporting characters with no independent motivation
4. Twist without setup — reveal introduces information withheld from the reader
5. Abrupt ending — resolution arrives before emotional payoff is delivered
6. Expertise gap — technical errors in investigative domain knowledge
7. Excessive gore — gore without atmosphere; shock without dread
8. Emotional absence — puzzle solved but reader feels nothing

### Opening Hook Types (original taxonomy)

- Question hook: opens with an unanswered question
- Conflict hook: opens in active opposition
- Suspense hook: known threat, unclear source
- Crisis hook: protagonist already under pressure
