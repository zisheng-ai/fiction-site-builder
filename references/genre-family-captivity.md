# Family Captivity and Erased Identity

Use this reference for wealthy-family confinement, passport seizure, identity erasure, forced impersonation, hidden rooms, secret dependents, or an estate that functions as a private prison.

## Story Promise

The protagonist lives inside a family narrative that has deleted her legal and social existence. The story must move from physical containment to documentary recovery, then to a public moment where the family can no longer control who is recognized. Escape alone leaves the central crime intact.

## Conflict Engine

- Map the perimeter: gates, staff, vehicles, devices, cameras, accounts, medication, visitors, and document control.
- Give confinement a public explanation such as illness, protection, grief, rehab, scandal management, or a voluntary retreat.
- Split authority among the public patriarch or matriarch, household operator, legal fixer, and conflicted heir.
- Let the protagonist build leverage from routines, inconsistencies, trusted staff, and records—not unexplained hacking skill.
- Keep the potential love interest useful but compromised; access does not equal rescue or innocence.

## Escalation Architecture

1. Open when the protagonist is forced to answer to another name or appear in a staged family event.
2. She discovers that a document, account, or outside person proves she still exists.
3. Her first escape attempt reveals the public cover story is already active.
4. A household ally changes sides and loses something material.
5. Midpoint: she reaches the outside, but authorities return or discredit her under falsified records.
6. She chooses to re-enter or maintain contact to recover another captive or the original identity chain.
7. Transfer control of a gate, server, trust vote, or live event.
8. Climax where the substitute identity and real identity must appear in the same public record.

## Character System

Use the erased protagonist, public replacement or heir, family controller, household operator, legal or medical fixer, conflicted insider, outside verifier, and another person whose freedom conflicts with immediate escape.

## Evidence and Public Payoff

Use layered identity proof: biometrics or DNA where appropriate, contemporaneous records, financial activity, witnesses, device history, and motive. The public reversal should make continued denial more costly than admission.

## Safety and Failure Modes

- Do not romanticize captivity or treat surveillance as devotion.
- Avoid a single unlocked door ending a distributed control system.
- Do not make staff uniformly loyal or evil; employment, fear, dependency, and guilt should conflict.
- Keep escape tactics plausible and avoid instructional detail that enables real-world harm.

## Quality Gate

The story passes when the estate's control system is coherent, the heroine changes strategy after each failed exit, identity proof is corroborated, and the ending restores mobility, documents, voice, and legal standing.
