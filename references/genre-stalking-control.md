# Stalking, Surveillance, and Identity Control

Use this reference for obsessive stalking, intimate surveillance, device compromise, impersonation, identity theft, or an offender manufacturing the appearance of a relationship.

## Story Promise

The antagonist tries to control not only where the protagonist goes but what other people believe about her. The thriller must restore credible authorship of her own life. A physical confrontation is insufficient if the forged social and digital record remains.

## Conflict Engine

- Choose a realistic access vector without turning the prose into a how-to manual: shared accounts, workplace privilege, data broker exposure, installed devices, social engineering, or trusted-person access.
- Make each intrusion serve a strategic purpose: isolate, discredit, predict, impersonate, or force contact.
- Separate the stalker's visible persona from the infrastructure or enablers that extend reach.
- Give the protagonist a safety plan with tradeoffs; changing devices or homes should cost access, work, evidence, or relationships.
- Avoid making the romantic lead's possessiveness mirror the stalker as an attractive alternative.

## Escalation Architecture

1. Open with proof that a private action has been observed or publicly rewritten.
2. The protagonist tests who knows a controlled fact.
3. Authorities or friends misread forged evidence as relationship conflict.
4. A safety measure triggers retaliation against reputation or employment.
5. Midpoint: the stalker is not merely watching but acting under her identity.
6. An ally is compromised, falsely accused, or revealed as an access source.
7. The protagonist creates a consented, legally safe verification trap focused on attribution.
8. Climax in a setting where the false relationship narrative collides with authenticated records and witnesses.

## Character System

Use the targeted protagonist, stalker or impersonator, skeptical authority, digital or security specialist, friend with compromised access, workplace or family gatekeeper, prior target, and ally whose protective instinct sometimes conflicts with autonomy.

## Evidence and Public Payoff

Preserve timestamps, device custody, account logs, witnesses, and behavior patterns. Avoid magical IP-address certainty. The payoff should restore the protagonist's credibility and sever access across legal, social, and technical layers.

## Safety and Failure Modes

- Do not publish actionable surveillance or evasion instructions.
- Do not frame stalking as proof of passion.
- Avoid victim-blaming through security perfection; ordinary trust is not consent to abuse.
- Do not make police either omnipotent or uniformly dismissive.

## Quality Gate

The story passes when intrusions escalate strategically, safety choices remain protagonist-led, attribution is corroborated, and the antagonist loses both access and narrative control.
